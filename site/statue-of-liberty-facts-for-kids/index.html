Statue of Liberty Facts for Kids

Embark on an exciting exploration of one of the most iconic symbols of freedom and democracy in the world – the Statue of Liberty! Standing tall in New York Harbor, this colossal statue is not just a monument but a beacon of hope and a symbol of welcome to millions. Gifted by France to the United States in 1886, the Statue of Liberty has a rich history and fascinating features that many are unaware of. Perfect for young historians and curious minds, these 30 fun facts about the Statue of Liberty will unveil the secrets and stories behind this majestic statue. So, let’s set sail on a journey of discovery to learn more about Lady Liberty and her significance!

30 Statue of Liberty Fun Facts for Kids

  1. The Statue of Liberty was a gift from France to the United States in 1886.
  2. Its full name is “Liberty Enlightening the World.”
  3. The statue was designed by French sculptor Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi.
  4. It was shipped to the U.S. in 350 individual pieces and assembled on site.
  5. The statue stands on Liberty Island in New York Harbor.
  6. Lady Liberty is made of copper and was originally a dull brown color before turning green due to oxidation.
  7. The statue’s green color is called a patina, which formed naturally over 20 years.
  8. The statue is a representation of the Roman goddess Libertas.
  9. She holds a torch above her head with her right hand, and a tablet inscribed with the date of the American Declaration of Independence in her left.
  10. The seven spikes on her crown represent the seven continents and seven seas.
  11. The statue is 151 feet tall, but with the pedestal and foundation, it stands at 305 feet.
  12. The Statue of Liberty was the tallest iron structure when it was completed.
  13. Visitors can climb 354 steps to reach the statue’s crown.
  14. The torch was replaced in 1986 with a new one covered in 24k gold leaf.
  15. Gustave Eiffel, who later designed the Eiffel Tower, engineered the statue’s internal structure.
  16. Inside the pedestal, there is a museum that tells the story of the statue.
  17. The original torch is now on display in the statue’s pedestal museum.
  18. The tablet in Lady Liberty’s hand measures 23 feet 7 inches tall and 13 feet 7 inches wide.
  19. The statue was designated as a National Monument in 1924.
  20. It became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984.
  21. The face of the Statue of Liberty is said to be modeled after the sculptor Bartholdi’s mother.
  22. There are 25 windows in the crown, symbolizing gemstones found on the earth and heaven’s rays shining over the world.
  23. The statue was used as a lighthouse for 16 years, from 1886 to 1902.
  24. The statue’s original torch had a flame covered in gold leaf.
  25. There are replicas of the Statue of Liberty in Paris and other cities around the world.
  26. The statue is struck by lightning multiple times each year.
  27. More than 4 million people visit the Statue of Liberty every year.
  28. The statue underwent major restoration for its centennial in 1986.
  29. The Statue of Liberty is one of the most recognizable symbols in the world.
  30. The statue is a popular site for protests and political demonstrations because of its symbol of freedom.
