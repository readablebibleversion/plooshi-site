Grand Canyon Facts for Kids

The Grand Canyon is a massive canyon carved by the Colorado River over millions of years. It’s known for its stunning views, unique geological features, and rich history. People from all over the world come to see its breathtaking beauty and learn about the natural processes that shaped it. Ready to find out more about this grand place? Here we go!

30 Fun Facts About the Grand Canyon

  1. The Grand Canyon is over 277 miles (446 km) long.
  2. At its widest point, it’s about 18 miles (29 km) wide.
  3. The canyon is more than a mile (1.6 km) deep in some places.
  4. The Colorado River flows through the canyon, carving it deeper over time.
  5. The Grand Canyon is one of the Seven Natural Wonders of the World.
  6. It’s located in the state of Arizona, in the southwestern United States.
  7. Over 5 million people visit the Grand Canyon each year.
  8. The canyon is home to over 1,000 plant species, 447 bird species, 91 mammal species, and 18 fish species.
  9. Grand Canyon National Park was established in 1919.
  10. The rocks at the bottom of the canyon are nearly 2 billion years old.
  11. Native American tribes, including the Havasupai, Hopi, Hualapai, Navajo, and Paiute, have lived in and around the canyon for thousands of years.
  12. The canyon offers activities like hiking, rafting, and helicopter tours.
  13. President Theodore Roosevelt was a major proponent of preserving the Grand Canyon.
  14. The Grand Canyon has its own weather system; the temperature can vary greatly between the top and the bottom.
  15. The Skywalk, a glass bridge that extends over the canyon, offers breathtaking views.
  16. There are more than 400 miles (644 km) of trails for hikers and mule riders.
  17. The North Rim and South Rim are the two main areas visited by tourists, but the North Rim is closed during winter due to snow.
  18. The Grand Canyon Railway offers a scenic train ride to the South Rim.
  19. Phantom Ranch is the only lodging below the rim of the canyon.
  20. The Grand Canyon was formed by erosion, caused primarily by water and ice, over millions of years.
  21. The Kaibab Plateau is the highest point in the park, at over 9,000 feet (2,743 meters) above sea level.
  22. The canyon’s colors change throughout the day as the sun moves across the sky.
  23. Supai Village, located at the bottom of the canyon, is one of the most remote communities in the continental United States.
  24. The California condor, one of North America’s most endangered birds, can be spotted in the park.
  25. The Grand Canyon has been featured in many movies and TV shows.
  26. Havasu Falls, located on the Havasupai Indian Reservation, is famous for its blue-green waterfalls.
  27. The Grand Canyon is a popular spot for stargazing due to its dark skies.
  28. Fossils found in the canyon include marine fossils and dinosaur tracks.
  29. The Colorado River has played a key role in shaping the canyon’s depth and width.
  30. The Grand Canyon is considered sacred by many Native American tribes, with numerous cultural sites throughout the park.

Conclusion

The Grand Canyon is not just a giant hole in the ground; it’s a place of beauty, history, and mystery. Whether you’re marveling at its vast size, learning about its ancient rocks, or exploring its diverse ecosystems, the Grand Canyon has something to amaze everyone. Remember these fun facts, and who knows, maybe one day you’ll visit the Grand Canyon and see its wonders for yourself!

FAQ

Q: How old is the Grand Canyon? A: The Grand Canyon is believed to be around 5 to 6 million years old, based on the age of the rocks and the erosion caused by the Colorado River.
